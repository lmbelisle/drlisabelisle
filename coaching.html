Physician Leadership Coaching

Coaching for
physician leaders.

Coaching for physicians stepping into leadership, and for those already leading. Lisa has made the move herself, and coaches from a doctoral framework for leading through change.

Who this is for

Lisa coaches physicians and advanced practice providers at two turning points, and often at both at once.

Stepping into leadership. New and rising physician leaders, medical directors, chiefs, service-line leaders, and physician executives, along with the physician-administrator dyads and interprofessional teams that run most health systems. The step from clinician to leader is the hardest transition in a medical career, and the least supported.

Building a different professional life. Physicians and advanced practice providers shaping a consulting, advisory, or thought-leadership path, or moving toward nonclinical work, on the expertise they already have. The work is naming what that record adds up to, and positioning it for the people and organizations who hire for it.

Lisa coaches nurse practitioners and physician assistants as well as physicians, work she knows well from years of supervising and developing advanced practice colleagues. Engagements can be arranged with the clinician directly or sponsored by the organization they lead.

The approach

Lisa coaches from roles she has held. She has directed primary care service lines, been medical director of a virtual-first primary and urgent care company, sat on the board of an accountable care organization, and led virtual care transformation as an Associate Chief Medical Officer. Her coaching draws on that operating experience and on a doctoral framework for leading through change.

She also brings two things most coaches cannot. She interviews people for a living on Radio Maine, so she knows how to help someone find the clear version of their story and deliver it well. And she has sat inside health systems deciding who to bring in, which is the view her clients need as they step into visibility and authority.

How the coaching works

Coaching runs as a defined series with a clear beginning and end. Before the first session, each client completes a short questionnaire that surfaces where they are and where they want to go. From there the work happens in the sessions and in a shared working document the two build together, so goals, positioning, and plans take shape on the page as they go. Between sessions, clients have email and voice-message access for quick questions.

What clients work on

Every engagement is shaped to the person, and often includes:

Authority
Stepping into leadership
Leading former peers, holding a room, and driving change through a system. The move from being the expert in the case to leading the people who make the decisions.
The shift
Clinician to leader
The mindset, habits, and skills the new role asks for, and the ones from clinical practice that no longer serve. This is the transition Lisa has made and studied.
Dyads and teams
Leading the dyad and the team
The physician-administrator dyad and the interprofessional teams beneath it. When the clinical lead and the operations lead pull in different directions, the work below them stalls.
Identity
Positioning and narrative
Turning a wide record into one clear story for the roles and rooms you are moving toward, from a sharp introduction to a full professional identity.
A path of your own
Consulting and thought leadership
For those building a consulting, advisory, or nonclinical direction: choosing the roles and engagements worth aiming at, and how to reach the people and organizations that hire for them.
Presence
Communication and presence
How you show up, speak, and are heard, in the room and on camera. Lisa brings years of interviewing and public speaking to this work.
How engagements work

Coaching typically begins with a focused block over about three months, with the option to continue. It runs as a set series with a clear beginning and end. Every engagement is scoped to the person and the goal, with the work, the time, and the cost set out up front. The best first step is a conversation.
