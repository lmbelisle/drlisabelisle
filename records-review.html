Physician consultations with Dr. Lisa Belisle

Making sense of your health information.

The Results Review and the Whole-Record Review.

A patient once came to her appointment with several folders of information on a subject I knew little about. I appreciated her thoroughness. I also felt overwhelmed, because reading it carefully meant fitting it into the time my health system allowed for her visit.

Today those folders might hold a private lab panel, a scan report, years of results from a patient portal or a long answer from an AI tool. Reviewing them properly still takes more time than most appointments allow. I now offer that time as a consultation of its own.

My aim is for you to walk into your next appointment knowing what you want to ask and why it matters to you. I review what you send, talk it through with you by video and send you a letter. You come away with what your records show, what is not in them and the questions you may want to bring to your own clinician, along with a one-page summary you can give them. I am not providing a diagnosis or suggesting a course of action. What to do next is for you and your clinician to decide.

I am a family physician, licensed in Maine and board-certified in Family Medicine and Preventive Medicine. I host Radio Maine, a video podcast of conversations with Maine’s artists, healers and community leaders. For several years, as part of my leadership, coaching and mentoring work, I’ve used AI myself and helped clinicians adopt it.

Two reviews

Results Review

A Results Review is for one set of results, such as a lab panel or a scan report, and the question you most want answered about it. If you have already asked an AI tool what the results mean, send that conversation too. In the review, its answer is set beside the results themselves, your history and your medication list, and I show you where it fits your record and where it does not. We talk for forty-five minutes. You receive a one-page letter, a page of questions to bring to your clinician and a one-page summary for your clinician.

Whole-Record Review

A Whole-Record Review is for someone whose records come from more than one clinician or more than one portal, or for a family helping a parent. You send the full export from your patient portal, up to two outside reports, a list of everything you take and any AI conversations you have had. I also ask about anything you measure yourself, such as readings from a watch, a ring or a blood pressure cuff. We talk for an hour.

The letter answers the questions you came with. It begins with what is already working and what has already been checked. It sets what you have told me beside what your records contain, because the two often differ, and it lists what is not in your records with the most relevant first. The questions for your clinician are written in your voice, so you can read them aloud, and under each is a short note for your clinician on why it is there. A separate page lists entries in your record that you may want to ask your clinician’s office to update.

Everything after that is optional. One page shows what is done and what is open in prevention, including what does not need a visit. Another is about the parts of your life that are not in your records. A third explains where your other health records are kept, from your pharmacy to your watch, and how to keep a record of your own. The last pages explain how the review was done and where to read further.

How I use AI

After removing your name and other identifying details, I run your records through the tools I use as a clinician. The questions I ask them come from my context as a physician, so the prompt is different from the one a patient would enter. Each claim a tool makes has to be traced to a page of your record before I consider it for your letter. AI helps draft the letter. I decide what goes into it, edit it and approve it before it reaches you. A page that comes with your letter explains which tools were used and how the claims were checked.

What to expect

You send what you have, as it is, through a private upload link. Before we talk I send you a short set of questions. The letter and its pages arrive within two business days of our call, and you may send me one follow-up question afterward. About a month later I write once to ask whether you saw your clinician and whether the questions were useful. I do not sell tests, supplements or memberships, and I do not prescribe, order tests or take the place of your clinician.

If I see a result that should not wait for your next appointment, I will tell you that day. Not hearing from me does not mean everything is fine.

For now, reviews are for adults who are in Maine at the time of our call and who send their own records. I take a limited number of reviews each month. If you have an appointment coming up, write as early as you can.

I am also licensed in Ohio and South Carolina and plan to offer reviews there. If you are in one of those states, write to me and I will let you know when they open. If you are somewhere else, you are welcome to write, and I will keep your name in case that changes.

Fees and refunds

A Results Review is $750 and covers up to 40 pages. A Whole-Record Review is $2,250 and covers up to 600. If you have more, I will send you a price before I begin. You pay when you book. If you change your mind before you send your records, I will refund the fee in full. If you complete the review and find it gave you nothing new to bring to your clinician, tell me within two weeks of receiving your letter and I will refund the fee.

Common questions

Do you take insurance?

No. I do not bill insurance, and you pay me directly. If you are enrolled in Medicare, please tell me when you write. Medicare requires a separate written agreement before I can work with you.

Is this a second opinion?

No. A second opinion gives a diagnosis or a treatment recommendation. I review what your records show, what is not in them and what you might ask your own clinician.

Will you tell me what to do?

No. I am not providing a diagnosis or suggesting a course of action. What to do next is for you and your clinician to decide.

Can you check what an AI tool told me about my results?

Yes. Send the conversation with your results. I show you where its answer fits your record and where it does not.

Can you review results I ordered myself, or a scan report?

Yes. A Results Review covers one lab panel or one scan report, whoever ordered it.

Who can book a review?

For now, adults who are in Maine at the time of our call and who send their own records. A parent books for themselves, and a family member is welcome on the call.

What happens to my records?

Your files go into a private folder that I control. I remove your name and other identifying details before any AI tool sees them, and I delete your source files ninety days after I send your letter.

How long does it take?

The letter and its pages arrive within two business days of our call.

To ask about a review, write to me at lbelisle@littlejohnisland.me. Please do not send records by email.
