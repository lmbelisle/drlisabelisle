Episode summary

Maine entrepreneur Les Otten, Maine Medical Center integrative medicine director Dr. Craig Schneider, brand strategist Linda Varrell, and Maine Youth Leadership's Frankee Chapa joined Dr. Lisa Belisle on Love Maine Radio for a conversation about fresh ideas. Otten reflected on the value of stepping back from a familiar situation and opening your eyes to the opportunity already present in front of you. Dr. Schneider described the work of helping patients sort through the noise of online health information, evaluating conventional and alternative therapies and translating findings into language a non-clinician can use. Varrell spoke about her communications work in Maine, and Chapa about the youth leadership organization shaping young people across the state. With co-host Genevieve Morgan, Dr. Belisle returned to a Maine Magazine Wellness issue profile that paired her with Dr. Schneider years earlier in an article called An Integrated Life, and to the way openness to disruption tends to be how genuinely new ideas arrive in a medical practice.

Dr. Craig Schneider:

Well, integrative medicine means a lot of different things to a lot of different people. When I think of integrative medicine myself, though, I think of relationship centered care where the physician and the patient have communication that allows them both to be part of planning and where options can come from multiple medical systems. It doesn't all have to be from conventional medicine, but also that there's a good information behind recommendations or suggestions. Like, I do believe that there are many things out there that aren't really based in good science or good evidence that I wouldn't necessarily recommend to patients, but that are definitely out in the community. I feel like my responsibility is to help educate as best I can patients, and then together we come up with a plan. And it usually does encompass many of the things that you just spoke about. Healthy eating, healthy relationships, healthy physical activity, healthy stress management, healthy sleep, healthy environment. I mean, all of the things that we can control. You know, helping people sort of take back some control in their lives. But at the same time, it seems like there's information overload out there. And so even though a lot of this is on the web, it's mixed in. There's good information mixed in with bad information, quite frankly. And so I think one of our jobs as physicians is to help people sort through that. And so I think a lot of what we do in training is really figure out how do you evaluate therapies, whether they're conventional or alternative, and then put that into language that people who may not have a medical background can understand.
