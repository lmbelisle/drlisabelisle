Episode summary

Carol Schoneberg, a Maine hospice educator since 1992 and longtime end-of-life educator, bereavement services manager, and grief counselor at Hospice of Southern Maine, joined Dr. Lisa Belisle on Love Maine Radio for a conversation about how grief is held in community. Schoneberg, who has been with Hospice of Southern Maine since its founding in 2004 as the state's only freestanding nonprofit hospice, described how the work found her early in life and how she came to understand it as a calling. She traced that sense back to her early twenties in San Francisco, when a friend took his life and her circle sat together in the strange silence afterward. She reflected on why people resist talking about grief, the assumption that it is something only negative, and the meaning she has drawn from sitting with families at the end of life. The conversation moved through vocation, loss, listening, and the steady presence grief asks of those who stay.
